Location sound: the mistake that most gives away an amateur short

Microphones, ambience, room tone and on-set habits to record clean dialogue. Why audiences forgive an imperfect image but not bad sound.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

Audiences forgive a grainy image, a crooked frame or an odd color. They do not forgive dialogue they cannot understand. Location sound is the technical area that most separates a professional short from an amateur one, and the one that gets the least attention in pre-production.

The right microphone

  • Shotgun microphone on a boom pole, pointing at the actor's mouth from above and out of frame. It is the foundation of film sound.
  • Wireless lavalier microphones, hidden in clothing. Useful in wide shots or when you cannot get the boom close.
  • The camera's microphone is no good for dialogue: use it only as a reference for syncing.

Ideally, record boom and lavalier at the same time on an external recorder.

Distance: the most important rule

The microphone should be as close as possible to the mouth without entering the frame. Doubling the distance greatly lowers the voice level relative to the room noise. Ask the cinematographer for the frame line and get right up to it.

Levels

  • Record at 48 kHz and 24 bits or, if your recorder allows it, in 32-bit float, which forgives level mistakes.
  • Leave headroom: voice peaks should not hit the maximum.
  • Wear closed-back headphones at all times. If you did not hear it, you did not record it.

Enemies of sound

  • Fridges, air conditioners and fans: turn them off during the take. Leave your car keys in the fridge so you do not forget to turn it back on.
  • Traffic and planes: wait for them to pass. In Mexico City this means patience.
  • Empty rooms: they echo. Put blankets, cushions or clothes out of frame.
  • Wind: use a synthetic fur windscreen outdoors.
  • Noisy clothing: synthetic fabrics that rub against the lavalier.

Room tone and wild tracks

When you finish each location, ask for total silence and record one minute of ambience of the space. The editor needs it to fill gaps between lines. Also record wild tracks: lines from the actors without camera, in case a line in the good take came out noisy.

Slate

Use a slate (even an app) or a clear clap at the start of each take. Syncing external sound without a reference means losing hours in the edit.

AI in post-production

AI audio cleanup tools help a lot with noise reduction. But they do not work miracles: dialogue recorded far away and with echo still sounds far away and with echo. Record well and use AI to polish.

Dialogue replacement (ADR)

If a take is unrecoverable, the dialogue can be re-recorded in a studio. It is expensive and hard to match. And remember: if you plan to use AI to recreate an actor's voice, Mexican law requires their written authorization.

Assign one person to sound only. If the person operating the camera is also recording audio, one of the two will come out badly.
