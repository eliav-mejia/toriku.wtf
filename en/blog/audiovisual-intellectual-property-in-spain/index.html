Audiovisual intellectual property in Spain: a guide for Latin American creators

Authors of an audiovisual work under Spain's LPI, assignment to the producer, collecting societies (SGAE, DAMA, AISGE, EGEDA), the quotation right and Spain's new AI law.

November 15, 2026 · 3 min read · Toriku team

Spain is a natural market for Mexican cinema: same language, important festivals and co-production funds. But its intellectual property law has differences worth knowing before you sign with a Spanish production company or platform.

The applicable law

The main statute is the Consolidated Text of the Intellectual Property Law (LPI, Royal Legislative Decree 1/1996), complemented by European directives.

Who the authors are

The LPI considers the authors of an audiovisual work to be:

  • The director.
  • The authors of the story, adaptation, script and dialogue.
  • The authors of the musical compositions created specifically for the work.

Unlike Mexico, the cinematographer does not appear on that list as an author of the audiovisual work.

Assignment to the producer and remuneration

The law presumes, unless otherwise agreed, that the authors assign the work's exploitation rights to the producer. But authors keep remuneration rights for certain uses, such as public screening or communication to the public, which are collected through collecting societies. This is an important difference from the US model.

Collecting societies

  • SGAE: authors, mainly music and some audiovisual authors.
  • DAMA: audiovisual directors and screenwriters.
  • AISGE: actors, dubbing artists, dancers and stage directors.
  • EGEDA: audiovisual producers.

If your work is screened or broadcast in Spain, it may generate rights that these societies collect. Check with the society for your trade whether you can join or whether there is a reciprocity agreement with a Mexican society.

Term

In Spain, as in the rest of the European Union, exploitation rights generally last the author's life plus seventy years. In Mexico it is one hundred. The same work can be in the public domain in one country and protected in another.

The quotation right

The LPI allows including fragments of other people's works for analysis, commentary or critical judgment, for teaching or research purposes, citing the source and the author, and to the extent justified by that purpose. It is the legal basis for many video essays in Spain, although its limits are strict.

Text and data mining

European rules allow those training AI models to mine lawfully accessible works, unless the rights holder has reserved that right appropriately (for example, in machine-readable form on a website). If you do not want your works used for training, say so that way on your website and in your contracts.

AI in Spain

In addition to the EU AI Act, whose deepfake transparency obligations apply from August 2, 2026, the Spanish government approved in May 2026 a draft organic law on the good use and governance of AI, with mandatory labeling of generated content and high penalties. As of November 2026 it is still going through parliament, so its text may change.

Registration

Spain has a voluntary Intellectual Property Registry. As in Mexico, it is not required for the work to be protected, but it serves as evidence.

If you co-produce with Spain, the contracts must say which law applies and which remuneration each author keeps. It is the most important conversation before the first day of shooting.

General information, not legal advice. Laws change and every case is different. Before signing, screening or publishing, consult an intellectual property lawyer in your country.
