Shooting a film on your phone: settings, accessories and workflow

How to get a cinematic image from a phone: manual mode, frame rate, shutter, color profiles, stabilization, sound and editing.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

There are films shot on phones that have made it to major festivals. The phone is not the limit; how you use it is. This is a practical guide to shooting a short on a phone that does not look like a home video.

Basic settings

  • Use an app with manual control (or the phone's pro mode). Auto mode changes exposure and color mid-take.
  • 24 frames per second for a cinematic look. 30 if your destination is only social media; 60 or more if you plan slow motion.
  • Shutter at double the frame rate: 1/48 or 1/50 at 24 fps. It gives natural motion.
  • ISO as low as possible: the small sensor gets noisy fast.
  • Lock focus and exposure before recording.
  • Fixed white balance: 5600 K in daylight, 3200 K under tungsten, or whatever you decide.

ND filters

In full sun, to keep the shutter at 1/50 you need to reduce the light coming in. A neutral density (ND) filter that clips over the lens is the most useful accessory for shooting outdoors.

Color profile

If your phone offers a log or flat profile, use it when you are going to grade: it keeps more information in highlights and shadows. If you are not going to grade, a well-exposed standard profile is better.

Stability

  • Tripod for static shots: cinema lives on still shots.
  • Three-axis gimbal for movement.
  • Avoid digital zoom: move closer physically.

Lenses

Switch between the phone's cameras with intent (wide, normal, telephoto) and stay consistent within a scene. Add-on anamorphic lenses give a distinctive widescreen format, but they take practice.

Sound: do not use the phone's microphone

Connect an external microphone or, better, record on a separate recorder with a shotgun or lavalier microphone. Read our note on location sound: it is what most gives away an amateur short.

Power and storage

  • An external battery always connected.
  • Airplane mode during the take.
  • Enough free space: high-quality video takes up a lot.
  • Offload and back up the footage at the end of each day in two places.

Editing workflow

  1. Copy the footage to the computer without recompressing.
  2. If the phone recorded at a variable frame rate, convert it to a constant rate before editing to avoid sound drift.
  3. Edit, grade and mix as with any camera.
  4. Export for the destination (see our note on delivery standards).

Vertical or horizontal

Decide before you shoot. If you are going to make both versions, frame with room to crop, or shoot the key takes twice. A short meant for festivals is horizontal; its trailer for social media can be vertical.

The phone takes away the excuse of not having a camera. What remains is what matters: story, light, sound and performance.
