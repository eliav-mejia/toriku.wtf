AI-made works in Mexico: what the Supreme Court decided

The SCJN's Amparo directo 6/2025 and the 2026 copyright reform on voice and likeness: what can be registered, what cannot and how to protect your work.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

In 2025, the Second Chamber of Mexico's Supreme Court of Justice (SCJN) decided the first Mexican case on works generated by artificial intelligence. In 2026 a legal reform on voice and likeness followed. Together they set the rules of the game for creators.

The case

A person tried to register with INDAUTOR a virtual avatar created with an image generation platform. The institute refused the registration, the case reached the Court, and the Court upheld the refusal in Amparo directo 6/2025.

What the Court said

  • Only a natural person can be an author. The LFDA reserves authorship for human beings.
  • AI has no creativity in the legal sense: there is no person making the creative decisions the law protects.
  • Therefore, a work generated exclusively by AI cannot be registered as a protected work.

Several commentators have pointed out that, in practice, that kind of content is left without a copyright holder.

What the Court did not say

The case was about an image generated from prompts. It did not settle every in-between scenario. It is still reasonable to argue that what a person contributes is protected:

  • A script you wrote in which you used AI to fix spelling.
  • A short shot by people with AI-generated effects.
  • The creative selection, arrangement and editing of material, even if part of it was generated.

The key is being able to show the human decisions.

The 2026 reform: voice and likeness

In May 2026 a reform to the LFDA was published requiring prior written authorization to clone or identifiably reproduce a person's voice or image using artificial intelligence, with significant financial penalties. At the same time, the new Federal Law of Film and Audiovisual Media states that dubbing into national languages is done by people.

How to protect your work when you use AI

  1. Document your process: drafts, versions, screenshots, dated project files.
  2. Use AI as a tool, not as the author: the creative decisions should be yours and explainable.
  3. Register what is yours: the script, the final work, the original music.
  4. Disclose AI use when a registry, festival or platform asks. Hiding it can invalidate your application.
  5. Check the tool's terms: some claim licenses over what you generate or upload.
  6. Written consent from anyone whose voice or image you alter with AI.

And outside Mexico

Other countries reach similar conclusions. In the United States, the Copyright Office and the courts require human authorship, although they protect the human selection and modification of generated material. In the European Union, the originality test is also based on a person's creative choices. See our notes on Spain and the United States.

In Mexico, AI does not take away your rights over what you do. But it does not give you rights over what it does on its own either.

General information, not legal advice. Laws change and every case is different. Before signing, screening or publishing, consult an intellectual property lawyer in your country.
