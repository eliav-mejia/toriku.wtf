Movie releases in Mexico: November 2026

The films reaching Mexican cinemas in November 2026: Mexican cinema, big franchises, animation and documentaries. With notes for filmmakers.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

November is one of the busiest months for movie listings: holiday-season films kick off, big franchises arrive and Mexican cinema rides the momentum of the autumn festivals. This is our selection, with dates as reported by Mexican media.

Release dates change often. Always confirm with your cinema's listings before you go.

Mexican cinema

  • Atrápelo quien pueda (November 12). A romantic comedy directed by Alfonso Pineda Ulloa, starring Jaime Camil and Natalia Téllez, presented at the Morelia Film Festival. Read our full note.
  • Petardos y magnolias (week of November 12). A Mexican coming-of-age drama worth looking for in the cinemas that program it.

Mexican cinema now has a legal guarantee of a minimum 14-day run in theaters under the new Film Law. If you want a national film to stay in cinemas, going on the opening weekend helps the most.

Big releases

  • Godzilla Minus Zero (early November). The follow-up to the Japanese film that surprised the world with top-tier visual effects made on a budget far smaller than Hollywood's. For any filmmaker, a lesson in efficiency.
  • The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (November 19 or 20, depending on the source). The prequel centered on Haymitch. Read our note.
  • Ebenezer: A Christmas Carol, I Play Rocky and How to Rob a Bank (week of November 12), a mix of Christmas classic, film about filmmaking and heist thriller.
  • Klara and the Sun (early November), the adaptation of Kazuo Ishiguro's novel directed by Taika Waititi: a story about an artificial friend that, in the middle of the conversation about AI, becomes unavoidable.

Animation and family

  • The Cat in the Hat (early November).
  • Hexed (November 26), the month's Disney animated film.

Music on the big screen

  • Rammstein comes to cinemas with a film of their concerts in Mexico. Concert screenings are a good example of how cinemas are looking for new audiences.

What to watch as a filmmaker

  • Godzilla Minus Zero: how effects are designed to serve emotion, not spectacle.
  • Sunrise on the Reaping: how to build a prequel when the audience already knows the ending.
  • Atrápelo quien pueda: how Mexican romantic comedy performs in cinemas, one of the genres that sells the most tickets for national films.
  • Klara and the Sun: how to portray an artificial character without falling into the robot cliché.

And at home

November also brings releases on streaming platforms. With the new Film Law, platforms must have a visible section for Mexican cinema: a good time to explore the national catalog.

We will update this note with reviews as we see the films in cinemas.
