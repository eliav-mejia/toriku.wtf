US copyright for Mexican creators: registration, fair use and AI

How US copyright works for a Mexican short: registration, work made for hire, fair use, public domain and AI-assisted works.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

The United States is the largest audiovisual market in the world and home to the platforms where almost every short lives. Even if your work is Mexican, US copyright rules affect you every time you upload, sell or license there.

Your work is already protected

Thanks to international treaties, a Mexican work is protected in the United States without any formalities. But registration with the U.S. Copyright Office has practical advantages: for certain claims, such as statutory damages and attorney's fees, you need to have registered in time. If you plan to distribute there, register.

Work made for hire

In the United States, a work made by an employee, or certain commissioned works with a written contract, belong from the start to whoever commissioned them. If a US production company hires you, check whether your contract says work made for hire: it means you will not be the owner.

Fair use

Fair use allows using other people's works without permission in certain cases, such as criticism, commentary, parody, teaching or news. Courts weigh four factors:

  1. The purpose of the use and whether it is transformative.
  2. The nature of the original work.
  3. The amount used.
  4. The effect on the market for the original work.

It is not a list of percentages. Since the Supreme Court's decision in Warhol v. Goldsmith (2023), courts look more carefully at whether the new use has a genuinely different purpose from the original. For a video essay, that means: short excerpts, in service of your analysis.

Public domain

In the United States, works published more than 95 years ago enter the public domain every January 1. In 2026, works published in 1930 entered it. Careful: a film being in the public domain there does not mean it is in Mexico, where protection lasts longer.

AI-assisted works

  • The Copyright Office and the courts require human authorship. In Thaler v. Perlmutter (2025), the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals confirmed that a work created by a machine with no human involvement cannot be registered.
  • In 2025 the Office published a report concluding that prompts alone are usually not enough to be an author, but that human selection, arrangement and modification of generated material are protected.
  • When registering a work that contains AI-generated material, you must disclose it and exclude that part from the application.

Notice and takedown

US platforms operate under the DMCA notice and takedown system. If someone uploads your short without permission, you can ask for it to be taken down. If someone claims against you unfairly, you can file a counter-notice. Be precise: false notices have consequences.

Platforms and Content ID

YouTube and other platforms use automatic identification systems. An automatic claim is not a ruling: you can dispute it if you have a license or if your use is fair.

For the US market, think in three words: register, document, license. And if your project is big, hire an entertainment lawyer there.

General information, not legal advice. Laws change and every case is different. Before signing, screening or publishing, consult an intellectual property lawyer in your country.
