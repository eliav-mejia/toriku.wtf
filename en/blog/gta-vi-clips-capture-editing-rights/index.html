GTA VI clips for social media: capture, editing and rights

How to capture, edit and publish GTA VI clips in vertical and horizontal: console settings, editing, music, Content ID and Rockstar's policy.

November 15, 2026 · 3 min read · Toriku team

GTA VI will generate millions of clips. For yours to stand out you need good capture, editing with intent and care with rights. This is a practical guide for consoles, which is where the game launches.

Capture on console

PlayStation 5

  • In the Captures and Broadcasts settings, choose the clip resolution and format. If your TV supports it, turn on HDR only if you are going to edit in HDR; otherwise capture in SDR to avoid washed-out colors.
  • Use the Create button to save recent gameplay or start a new recording. Set in the settings how far back the console saves.

Xbox Series X|S

  • The Share button saves what just happened or starts a recording. In the Captures settings, choose the clip resolution and length.
  • Long captures usually require a lower resolution or external storage: check your console's options.

External capture card

For long clips, streams or maximum quality, a capture card connected to a computer gives you full control over codec and bitrate. Remember that HDCP-protected content cannot be captured; games let you turn it off in the console settings.

Photo mode and camera

If the game offers a photo mode or a replay editor, use it: it allows framing that is not possible while playing. Think like a cinematographer: camera height, focal length, rule of thirds and the in-game sunset light.

Editing

  • Hook in the first two seconds: the most surprising moment goes first.
  • Vertical (9:16) for Shorts, Reels and TikTok; horizontal (16:9) for YouTube and long analysis. Keep the action centered if you are going to crop.
  • On-screen subtitles: most people watch without sound.
  • Your voice: your own commentary turns a clip into content with value and protects you under inauthentic content policies.
  • Length: between 15 and 45 seconds for vertical clips; if it works, make the long version.

Music: the biggest risk

GTA is famous for its radio stations with commercial songs. Those songs have their own rights holders and are usually registered in systems like Content ID.

  • Turn off the in-game radio when capturing, or lower its volume in the audio settings.
  • If the game offers a creator option that mutes licensed music, turn it on.
  • Use your own or licensed music for your edits.

Rockstar's policy

Rockstar publishes on its support site a policy on using its material in videos and streams. Before monetizing, read it and respect what it says about:

  • Monetizing videos with game footage.
  • Leaked or pre-release material.
  • Using brands and logos in thumbnails.

Content ID and claims

  • A music claim can remove your video's monetization or mute parts of it.
  • YouTube lets you trim or replace the claimed part.
  • If your use is legitimate, you can dispute it; read our note on video essays and rights.

Format ideas

  1. Cinema in Vice City: recreate famous film shots inside the game.
  2. Real vs. game comparison: Miami locations against their Leonida version.
  3. Mission breakdown: what makes a scene work dramatically.
  4. Shooting diary: a short filmed with the game's photo mode or editor.

A good clip is not the one that shows the most of the game, but the one that best shows your eye. Play like a gamer, edit like a filmmaker.
