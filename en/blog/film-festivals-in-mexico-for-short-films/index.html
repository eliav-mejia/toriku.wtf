Film festivals in Mexico for short filmmakers: map and strategy

Morelia, Guadalajara, Guanajuato, FICUNAM, Ambulante, Shorts México and more: how to choose which festivals to send your short to, and in what order.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

Sending your short to every festival you find is expensive and almost never works. Strategy starts with understanding what each festival is looking for and what it requires regarding premieres.

The big Mexican festivals

  • Morelia International Film Festival (FICM), in October. Its Mexican short film section is one of the most important in the country, and its award, the Ojo, is highly respected.
  • Guadalajara International Film Festival (FICG), one of the oldest, with a strong industry and market presence.
  • Guanajuato International Film Festival (GIFF), with a long short film tradition and activities in several cities.
  • FICUNAM, focused on auteur cinema and risky proposals.
  • Ambulante, a documentary tour that travels through several states.
  • Shorts México, devoted exclusively to short film.
  • Los Cabos International Film Festival, with an industry and co-production profile.

There are also dozens of regional, themed (horror, science fiction, human rights, diversity) and university festivals that are excellent for premiering and gaining experience.

Order matters: premieres

Many festivals ask for a premiere: world, international, Latin American or national. If your short screens first at a small festival, it may lose the chance to get into a big one that requires a Mexican premiere.

A common strategy:

  1. Apply first to the top-tier international festivals you care about.
  2. Then to the big national ones.
  3. Then to the regional and themed circuit.
  4. Finally, publish online.

Qualifying awards

Some festivals qualify their short film winners for industry awards, such as the Oscars. The Academy publishes the list of qualifying festivals every year. If you dream of that route, check the list before deciding on your premiere.

How to choose

Ask yourself, for each festival:

  • Which shorts did it select last year? Are they like mine?
  • Does it require a premiere? What kind?
  • How much is the entry fee and when does it close?
  • Does it pay screening fees or give a cash prize?
  • Does it have industry activities where I can meet producers?

The festival kit

  • One-line and one-paragraph synopsis, in Spanish and English.
  • A short, honest director's statement.
  • High-resolution stills and poster.
  • A private, password-protected link with English subtitles.
  • Technical specs and full credits.
  • A projection file ready to go (see our note on DCP).

The case of Morelia 2026

At the 2026 FICM, the Ojo for best Mexican fiction short went to «Cuerda manual» (Manual Rope), by Gustavo Obregón. Watching the shorts that win is the best way to understand what each festival is looking for. Read our note on the winners.

A festival is not just a screen: it is a week to meet the people you will make your next project with. If you are selected, go.
