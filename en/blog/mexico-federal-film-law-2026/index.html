Mexico's 2026 Federal Law of Film and Audiovisual Media: what changes for creators

The law that replaces the 1992 one: a 10 % screen quota, 14 days in theaters, a Mexican cinema section on platforms and human dubbing.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

After 34 years, Mexico has a new film law. The Federal Law of Film and Audiovisual Media was approved by the Senate in April 2026 and replaces the 1992 Federal Cinematography Law. These are the changes that matter if you make films, shorts or audiovisual content.

1. A 10 % screen quota and 14 days

Cinemas must reserve 10 % of screening time for Mexican films, at fair showtimes. The law extends the minimum run of a Mexican film to 14 days. The idea is to stop a national release from disappearing from theaters after its first weekend.

2. Platforms: a section for Mexican cinema

Video-on-demand services must offer a section that guarantees the visibility of Mexican film and audiovisual work. For independent creators, this opens a concrete conversation with platforms: they need national catalog.

3. Dubbing done by people

The law states that dubbing of foreign works into national languages is done by human performers. It is direct protection for the Mexican dubbing industry, one of the most recognized in the Spanish-speaking world.

4. Voice and likeness versus AI

In parallel, a reform to the Federal Copyright Law published in May 2026 requires prior written authorization to clone or identifiably reproduce a person's voice and image with artificial intelligence, with significant fines for doing so without consent.

Practical consequence: every contract with actors, voice talent or models must say what can be done with their voice and image, including AI.

5. Film heritage

The law includes the obligation to rescue, restore and preserve the country's film and audiovisual heritage. For film clubs and programmers, this may mean more restored titles available under license.

What still needs watching

  • The regulations. Many operating rules depend on them: how the quota is measured, how platforms are supervised and which penalties apply.
  • Incentives. Check how support programs and production tax incentives fare under the new law.
  • The definition of «Mexican work». It determines who benefits from the quota and from the platform section.

What to do now if you produce

  1. Review your contracts and add an AI clause on voice, image and training.
  2. Register your works with INDAUTOR: it gives you certainty when negotiating with platforms.
  3. Get your catalog ready for platforms: subtitles, technical specs and a complete chain of title.
  4. If you work in dubbing or voice-over, read the full law in the Diario Oficial de la Federación, Mexico's official gazette.

Reading the full law takes an afternoon and saves you years of misunderstandings. Always check the text published in the DOF, not just press reports.

General information, not legal advice. Laws change and every case is different. Before signing, screening or publishing, consult an intellectual property lawyer in your country.
