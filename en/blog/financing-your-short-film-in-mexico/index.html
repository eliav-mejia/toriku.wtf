Financing your short in Mexico: public funds, incentives and alternatives

IMCINE and state calls, creation grants, tax incentives, co-production, brands and crowdfunding for Mexican short films.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

A short film can cost anything from almost nothing to hundreds of thousands of pesos. The difference is usually crew, locations and post-production. These are the real sources of money for a short in Mexico, and how to combine them.

1. IMCINE calls

The Mexican Film Institute publishes calls for production and post-production support every year, with categories that have included fiction, documentary and animated shorts. Requirements change with each edition, so always read the current call. In general they will ask for:

  • A complete script and director's statement.
  • A detailed budget and schedule.
  • A portfolio of previous work.
  • Chain-of-title documents.

2. State and municipal institutes

Several states have their own film funds or creation incentive programs. They usually have less competition than national calls and require you to shoot in, or be a resident of, the state. Check your state's culture ministry.

3. Creation grants

The Sistema de Apoyos a la Creación y Proyectos Culturales (formerly known as FONCA) has programs for young and established creators that have included audiovisual disciplines. It is a grant for the creator, not for the whole production, but it can cover a project's development.

4. Tax incentives

Mexico has had tax incentives for film production that let companies contribute to a film in exchange for a tax benefit. They have been designed mainly for features. With the new Federal Law of Film and Audiovisual Media, check how these instruments stand before planning around them.

5. Festivals with production funds

Some national and international festivals run labs and funds for projects in development. Besides money, they provide mentoring and contacts with producers.

6. Co-production

A short can be co-produced with another country. The short shot in Tepito that won the Palme d'Or at Cannes 2026, for example, was put together as an international co-production. Co-production opens funds in both countries, but it demands clear contracts from the start.

7. Brands and sponsorship

A brand can fund a short if the story connects with its values. See our note on monetization: negotiate ownership of the work and the right to take it to festivals in writing.

8. Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding campaigns work best when you already have a community. Three keys:

  • A short video that shows the project's tone.
  • Concrete rewards: a credit, a private screening, exclusive material.
  • A realistic goal and a daily promotion plan.

How to build the budget

  1. Development: script, scouting, casting.
  2. Production: crew, gear rental, locations, transport, catering, insurance.
  3. Post-production: editing, color, sound, music, subtitles, DCP.
  4. Distribution: festival entry fees, press materials, travel.

Many projects forget the fourth block and run out of money for anyone to see the short.

Public money asks for paperwork in order. Always keep your dossier up to date: registered script, budget, contracts and a clear synopsis. The next call is always closer than you think.
