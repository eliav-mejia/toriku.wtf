FICM 2026: what the Morelia winners leave us

«Seis meses en el edificio rosa con azul», «Cuerda manual» and «Forensita»: what Morelia 2026 rewarded and what short filmmakers can learn.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

The 24th Morelia International Film Festival, held in October 2026, once again showed where Mexican cinema is heading. These are some of the main awards and what they say about the moment.

The main awards

  • Best Mexican feature and Audience Award: Seis meses en el edificio rosa con azul (Six Months in the Pink and Blue Building), by Bruno Santamaría Razo. Winning both the jury award and the audience award is not common: it is a film that connected with critics and with people.
  • Best Mexican fiction short: Cuerda manual (Manual Rope), by Gustavo Obregón.
  • Best Mexican documentary feature: Forensita, by Pablo Vaca.

The full list, with animation, documentary shorts and special mentions, is on the festival's official site.

Three lessons for short filmmakers

1. The intimate travels far

The winning titles bet on close-up stories: a building, a family, a trade. They do not need large scale to talk about big subjects. It is the same logic as Para los contrincantes, the short about child boxing shot in Tepito that won the Palme d'Or for best short film at Cannes 2026, directed by Argentine filmmaker Federico Luis.

2. Documentary is more alive than ever

Mexican documentary remains one of the country's strengths. For a creator on a small budget, the documentary short is an excellent way in: it asks for time and access more than expensive gear.

3. The audience votes too

The Audience Award is a reminder that a festival is not just a jury. Full screenings, questions at the end and conversation on social media count. If your short gets into a festival, invite people, introduce the screening and stay for the Q&A.

Morelia is also industry

Beyond the competition, FICM works as a platform for commercial releases. In 2026, for example, the romantic comedy Atrápelo quien pueda, with Jaime Camil and Natalia Téllez, premiered there before its theatrical release in November.

How to prepare for the next edition

  1. Finish your short months in advance: calls close sooner than you think.
  2. Protect the premiere: Morelia values Mexican premieres.
  3. Prepare an impeccable kit (see our note on festivals).
  4. If you do not get in, ask for feedback when possible and keep going on the circuit.

Watching the winning shorts is the best free school there is. Look for them in the festival's touring screenings and on platforms when they are released.
