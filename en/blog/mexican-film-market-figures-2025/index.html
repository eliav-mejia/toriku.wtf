The film market in Mexico: 2025 figures and what they mean for a creator

Attendance, box office, insecurity and streaming: what IMCINE's yearbook says about Mexican cinema and how to read it if you make shorts.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

Mexico is one of the largest film markets in the world by tickets sold, with two big exhibition chains and abundant national production. But Mexican cinema takes a small share of that box office. Understanding the numbers helps you decide what to shoot and where to show it.

What the 2025 figures say

According to reports published on IMCINE's 2025 Statistical Yearbook of Mexican Cinema:

  • Mexican films sold around 9 million tickets in the year.
  • They grossed in the order of 640 million pesos at the box office.
  • That is less than 5 % of the country's total attendance and box office.
  • For the first time, the yearbook analyzes streaming platforms as direct competition for cinemas.

Figures vary slightly between outlets because of differences in method. To quote them in a project dossier, always use IMCINE's official PDF.

Why people go to the cinema less

Three factors keep coming up:

  1. Price: a family trip to the cinema costs more than a month of a platform.
  2. Insecurity: official surveys show that a significant share of the adult population stopped going to the cinema or theater for fear of being a victim of crime.
  3. Short windows: many films reach streaming a few weeks after release.

The legal response: quota and run length

Mexico's new 2026 Federal Law of Film and Audiovisual Media keeps a 10 % screen quota for Mexican films in cinemas, with fair showtimes and a minimum run of 14 days. It also requires platforms to give visibility to national cinema with a dedicated section. It is too early to measure the effect, but it changes the conversation with exhibitors and platforms.

What it means for short filmmakers

  • The short lives outside commercial cinemas. Festivals, film clubs, platforms and social media are its natural circuit, and there the competition is for attention, not screens.
  • Young audiences are on their phones. Think of your short in vertical too: a 30-second 9:16 trailer can bring more people to your festival screening than the poster.
  • Local stories work. Mexican cinema that connects tends to be specific: a neighborhood, a trade, a family. The short shot in Tepito that won the Palme d'Or at Cannes 2026 is a good example.
  • Comedy and horror sell tickets; auteur drama wins festivals. Decide what you want to achieve before you write.

Five numbers you should know about your own short

  1. What it cost, peso by peso.
  2. How many festivals selected it out of how many you applied to.
  3. How many people saw it in cinemas and online.
  4. How much direct income it generated.
  5. How much new work it brought you.

The Mexican market is tough for features and generous to those who know how to move in short formats. The numbers are not a verdict: they are a map.
