Writing a short film: structure in ten pages

How to write a short that works: a single idea, a character with a want, a twist and an ending that stays with you. Exercises and common mistakes.

November 15, 2026 · 2 min read · Toriku team

A short film is not a cut-down feature. It is a different form, with its own rules. By convention, one script page equals about one minute on screen, so ten pages make a short of around ten minutes: a comfortable length for festivals.

A single idea

The most common mistake is trying to tell too much. A good short can be summed up in one line:

  • Who wants what.
  • What stands in their way.
  • What changes at the end.

If you need three sentences to explain it, it is probably a feature or a series.

Structure in ten pages

  1. Page 1: the world and the want. In the first scene we know where we are, who the character is and what they want. There is no time for long introductions.
  2. Pages 2 to 4: the obstacle. Something complicates the want. Better if it is concrete and visual.
  3. Pages 5 to 7: the escalation. The character tries and fails, or gets something they did not expect.
  4. Pages 8 to 9: the twist. A revelation or a decision changes the meaning of what we saw.
  5. Page 10: the final image. A shot the audience takes home.

Show, don't explain

In a short every line of dialogue costs. Ask yourself in each scene: can this be told with an image? A character who looks at their phone and does not answer says more than a monologue about their relationship with their mother.

Write for your budget

  • Few locations: ideally one or two you can get for free.
  • Few characters: two or three with lines.
  • Available light: if you write night scenes, think about how you will light them.
  • No crowds, chases or rain if you have no way to pull them off.

Limitations do not kill creativity: they force it to show up.

Format

Use standard screenplay format: scene headings (INT./EXT., place, time), action in the present tense and centered dialogue. There are free programs that do it for you. A well-formatted script signals professionalism to actors and funders.

Common mistakes

  • Opening with an alarm clock going off.
  • Ending with «it was all a dream».
  • Characters telling each other what they both already know.
  • An ending that spells out the message.
  • Scenes that change nothing.

Exercise

Write three versions of your short in one line each. Then write only the last scene of each version. The one that moves you most is the one you should write in full.

Register before you share

Before you send your script to actors, funds or producers, register it with INDAUTOR, Mexico's copyright office. It is cheap and quick, and it gives you proof of date. Read our note on film copyright in Mexico.

A memorable short is not the one that tells the most, but the one that leaves a single image impossible to forget. Write toward that image.
