AI standards in the industry: unions, contracts and content credentials

What the WGA and SAG-AFTRA set out on AI, what C2PA content credentials are, and how to adopt those rules in Mexican productions.

November 15, 2026 · 3 min read · Toriku team

The industry did not wait for governments to regulate artificial intelligence. Hollywood unions negotiated rules in 2023, tech companies created an open standard to show where an image comes from, and Mexico legislated on voice and likeness in 2026. These are the standards that already shape how the work is done.

Screenwriters: the WGA agreement

The 2023 agreement between the Writers Guild of America and the studios established, in short:

  • AI is not a writer: AI-generated material does not count as literary material or source material.
  • A studio cannot require a writer to use AI.
  • A writer may use AI if the company allows it, and the company must disclose if it hands them AI-generated material.

The underlying idea works in any country: authorship and credit remain human.

Actors: the SAG-AFTRA agreement

The 2023 film and television agreement created rules on digital replicas:

  • Informed and specific consent from the actor to create and to use their replica.
  • Payment for the days the replica «works».
  • Special rules for replicas created from existing material.

Video game performers negotiated similar protections after a long strike that ended in 2025.

Mexico: voice and likeness in writing

In May 2026, reforms to the Federal Copyright Law were published requiring prior written authorization to clone or identifiably reproduce a person's voice or image with AI. The new Federal Law of Film and Audiovisual Media also states that dubbing into national languages is done by human performers. For a small production company this means an AI clause in every contract.

C2PA: the nutrition label of an image

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) is an open standard that stores, inside the file, a signed record of how an image or video was created and edited. It is known as content credentials.

  • Some professional cameras and recent phones already sign photos when they are taken.
  • Several editing programs add the record on export, including whether generative AI was used.
  • Platforms and social networks are starting to read those credentials to display a label.

For a creator, C2PA is a way to prove that their material is theirs and how they made it. It is especially useful in documentary and news photography.

Europe: labeling deepfakes

From August 2, 2026 the transparency obligations of the European Union AI Act apply: anyone publishing a deepfake must state that it was generated or manipulated, with nuanced exceptions for obviously artistic or fictional works. The machine-readable marking obligation for tool providers has a grace period until December 2026 for systems that were already on the market.

How to adopt it in an independent production

  1. AI clause in contracts with actors, voice talent, screenwriters and photographers.
  2. AI log: tool, scene and purpose.
  3. Clear credit when AI generates something the audience sees.
  4. Content credentials turned on when exporting stills and press material.
  5. Do not train models on footage without permission from the people who appear in it.

AI standards do not hold back creativity: they protect the people who make it possible. A production that adopts them early negotiates better with platforms, brands and festivals.
